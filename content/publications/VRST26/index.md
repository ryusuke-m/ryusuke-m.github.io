abstract: 'A volumetric display is a display type capable of presenting three-dimensional images in physical space. However, in many existing volumetric displays, it has been difficult for users to directly touch displayed virtual objects with their bare hands and perceive tactile and force-feedback sensations. We substantially extend the concept presented in our prior work and propose a volumetric display that uses a sweeping flexible screen to provide mechanical feedback spatially associated with displayed three-dimensional imagery. By touching the moving screen with their bare hands, users perceive physical contact at their fingertips and elastic resistance generated by the screen’s restoring force. Varying the scanning frequency and amplitude changes not only the temporal and mechanical characteristics of screen–finger contact, but also the visual quality and depth extent of the volumetric imagery, allowing the presentation to be adapted to the displayed content and the user’s interaction. We implemented a system that separately adjusts these scanning parameters and dynamically modulates them in response to the user’s pressing motion. Evaluations confirmed that volumetric images could be rendered under different scanning frequencies and amplitudes and that the measured reaction force increased within the tested operating range. A user study with a volumetric button indicated that the higher-frequency condition at a fixed amplitude was rated higher for the sense of touching the displayed object, while increasing the amplitude did not clearly improve perceived pushback or depth. The dynamic modulation profiles were not clearly distinguished in the subjective ratings. These results suggest that a swept bending screen can serve as both an optical diffuser and a screen-mediated physical interface for bare-hand reaction-force feedback.'
